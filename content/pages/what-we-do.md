## Accounting built around how your industry works

Bookkeeping software can log a transaction. It can't tell you why your job costs ran over budget in June, or how South Dakota's seasonal labor rules affect your third-quarter payroll withholding. Buss CPA built its outsourced accounting services around industries the firm already understands deeply: agriculture and ranching, construction, law, engineering, and family wealth management.

Every client pays a fixed monthly fee, so there's no surprise invoice when your books get more complicated during planting season, a busy construction stretch, or a complex trust distribution. You get secure digital workflows and real-time online access to your financial data, checked from a phone or laptop on your own schedule. The firm has invested in the technology infrastructure so you don't have to build one in-house.

With offices in Hartford and Sioux Falls, Buss CPA serves businesses and families across the greater Sioux Falls area and throughout South Dakota. These clients don't need someone to log entries. They need an outsourced accounting department that already understands job costing, seasonal cash flow, trust reporting, and the tax rules specific to their industry. That's what Buss CPA delivers, whether you run a grain operation, manage a cattle ranch, lead a construction crew, run a law practice, head an engineering firm, or oversee a multi-generational family office.

## Personal income tax and household financial support

Not every client who works with Buss CPA owns a business. Families and individuals across Sioux Falls, Hartford, and the surrounding communities come to the firm for the same reason business owners do: they want a straight answer and a return filed correctly the first time. Buss CPA handles W-2 filers, retirees managing required distributions, and households juggling rental property or investment income.

For business owners, personal and business tax planning happen together instead of in separate silos with separate deadlines. A construction company owner's personal return reflects decisions made in the business all year, and the firm is already tracking both sides. An agriculture client's Schedule F and personal return move together, because the decisions that affect one almost always affect the other. An attorney's compensation structure or an engineer's pass-through income flows directly into the personal return, and having the same team handle both eliminates the gaps that come from working with separate preparers. That coordination is hard to get from a firm that only sees your business books or only sees your 1040.
